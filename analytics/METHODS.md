# Analytics implementation notes

EpiScope is an educational prototype. Its encounter and stock data are synthetic, and its resource conversions express explicit planning assumptions. The code does not constitute clinical guidance or a validated national surveillance service. Actual results are written by each run to `results/analytics_latest.json` and the associated CSV files.

## Daily surveillance and reporting delays

The detector evaluates each district and disease separately. Its main signal counts cases by **onset date**, using only records received within seven days of onset. A decision about an onset cohort dated 1 June is therefore issued on 8 June. Every baseline cohort has the same maturity; a report received after that decision cannot retrospectively improve the signal. This deliberate waiting period reduces reporting artefacts but costs seven days of detection timeliness. The output retains both onset `date` and actual `as_of_date`.

EARS C2 compares the current cohort against the seven cohorts from days t−9 through t−3. A two-day guard interval reduces contamination by the beginning of a new rise. Its score is `(observed − baseline mean) / baseline sample SD`; the SD has a floor of one case, which is a disclosed low-count adaptation. The operational default is a score above three. All seven baseline cohorts must exist. Current reporting completeness is smoothed over the trailing seven days, and both its current value and baseline average must be at least 0.6; smoothing avoids treating every legitimate low-volume day as an outage. This follows the baseline structure in [CDC's EARS description](https://www.cdc.gov/Mmwr/pdf/wk/mm54su01.pdf).

The second method is a one-sided standardized CUSUM, `S[t] = max(0, S[t−1] + z[t] − 0.5)`, with a default decision limit of five. Missing or ineligible observations reset accumulation. The reference value and limit follow the common settings explained by [NIST](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/software/dataplot/refman1/auxillar/cusum.htm). A short local baseline can adapt to prolonged outbreaks and miss part of their duration; this is one reason both methods and their threshold curves are evaluated.

Spark window functions compute EARS baselines. Spark grouped pandas execution handles the short CUSUM recursion independently for each district–disease series. Neither routine reads the generator's truth directory.

A second safeguard addresses the depressed short baseline immediately after a reporting blackout. A district report day with at least ten records and more than 50% arriving over seven days after onset is flagged as a suspected backfill, using only the incoming feed. Alerts pause on that issue day and the next seven days; the quality flag is retained. This guard can delay a genuine concurrent outbreak, so its effect must be examined alongside sensitivity. It is a transparent reporting-quality rule rather than a learned outbreak classifier.

## Detection evaluation

Only `evaluate.py` reads outbreak or reporting-artefact truth. It measures unique-event sensitivity, district-event sensitivity, false alert episodes per district-year, and median delay from outbreak start to the actual alert issue date. A national event counts once in the first measure and once per affected district in the second. A timely hit must be issued before the event ends; retrospective attribution alone is insufficient.

A false alert episode begins after at least seven quiet days. The false-alert denominator is district-years, pooled across diseases. This convention is recorded with the results and should be retained when comparing methods. Threshold curves include a calibration period and a held-out final calendar year. A recommended threshold is selected only from the calibration curve subject to no more than five false episodes per district-year; the dashboard's fixed defaults remain explicit. The artefact evaluation also runs a naive report-date C2 comparator over the backfill window. Observed concurrent alerts are reported honestly rather than assumed to be zero.

## Weekly forecasts and uncertainty

Four separate direct models predict horizons one to four weeks for every district and all four required diseases. Each small model uses ridge regression on log case counts, recent counts, annual and semiannual harmonics, and rainfall, temperature and humidity at target-week lags four, six and eight. The minimum weather lag is at least the maximum horizon, so future weather is never supplied. Missing climate fields have explicit indicators and a neutral zero placeholder; scaling is fitted only on the training rows. This is an interpretable modelling choice, not a claim of biological causality.

Spark first aggregates encounters into onset-week/report-week cohorts. For every historical Sunday issue date, the model reconstructs recent case features from the reports actually available at that date. Training labels must be at least three weeks old, respecting the generator's maximum 21-day delay. Admission ratios use the same mature training period. The final incomplete calendar week is excluded. `origin` identifies the last complete observed week and `issued_on` its Sunday; horizon one starts the following Monday.

The rolling-origin test scores the last **52 fully mature target weeks**. The latest three weeks are excluded from scoring so right-censored outcomes are not treated as complete truth. Models are refitted at every origin. The benchmark predicts the count observed 52 weeks earlier, following the [seasonal-naive method](https://otexts.com/fpp2/simple-methods.html). Both methods receive intervals derived from earlier, already matured prequential forecast errors. The empirical 80th percentile of absolute errors forms symmetric, nonnegative 80% intervals. These intervals require calibration assessment; 80% is their nominal target rather than a guaranteed empirical coverage.

The outputs report MAE, coverage and a weighted interval score using one central interval plus the median. The implementation follows the proper-score formulation in [Bracher et al. (2021)](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008618): `(0.5 × absolute error + 0.1 × IS_0.2) / 1.5`. Lower MAE/WIS and coverage near 0.8 are desirable. A model is not declared superior before these measurements exist. Per-district tables and raw backtest predictions remain available for audit.

## Demand conversion and allocation

Four-week upper80 case forecasts support cautious procurement. The assumptions are two IV-saline units per dengue admission, 0.2 saline units per diarrhoeal case, one NS1 kit per dengue case, two ORS units per diarrhoeal case, one antimalarial course per malaria case, and one response-team equivalent per 500 forecast cases. Bed demand uses weekly upper80 admissions multiplied by a five-day average stay and divided by seven. The maximum weekly requirement per disease is combined as a conservative district capacity requirement. These are adjustable teaching assumptions, and should be validated with clinicians and supply managers before operational use. Adding marginal upper quantiles is conservative; it is not a jointly calibrated 80% national demand interval.

The LP minimizes weighted unmet demand after current local stocks. It enforces nonnegative allocation and unmet demand, a national purchasing budget, per-division transport capacity, and the population equity floor `x[d,r] ≥ alpha × p[d] × sum_d x[d,r]`. Thus alpha=0.2 reserves at least 20% of each resource's allocated total according to population; the rest may follow need. Unit costs, transport volumes and severity weights appear together in `RESOURCE_PARAMETERS`. Budget units are generic. Division transport capacities are fixed across scenarios at 110% of their initial net-demand transport requirement, with a small minimum. They express an explicit stress assumption rather than observed logistics infrastructure.

The comparator distributes resource budgets in proportion to national net need and then distributes each resource by population. It obeys the same budget and transport limits; if needed, a common scale factor preserves population shares while satisfying transport. The [SciPy HiGHS interface](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.optimize.linprog.html) solves the small 384-row district-resource programme. Driver-side optimization is appropriate because Spark has already reduced millions of encounter records to 64 districts × six resources.

The scenario grid contains six budget levels, from 10% to 125% of national net-need purchasing cost, and equity floors 0, 0.2, 0.5 and 0.8. Its measured efficiency–equity values are stored in `allocation_scenarios`. The default recommendation uses the 50% budget and 0.2 floor as a transparent starting policy. It is accompanied by measured unmet need, baseline comparison and transport use; it is not presented as a universally optimal policy. If stocks exceed need, zero purchases are a valid result. Bed and team quantities are continuous capacity equivalents; an integer dispatch model would be needed before assigning indivisible physical units.

## Preparedness calendar and provenance

The calendar aggregates weekly counts by division and disease. A centered annual moving average removes the trend from log counts; the remaining values are averaged by ISO epidemiological week and centered. Exponentiation yields a multiplicative seasonal index. Weeks above 1.2 receive a surge-readiness action; a threshold crossing two to four weeks ahead triggers pre-positioning and prevention. This is a classical trend/seasonal/remainder decomposition, with the 53rd ISO-week slot retained explicitly. It is a planning summary rather than a causal estimate.

Each completed run records source hashes, the input Parquet manifest fingerprint, observation date, stage timings and row counts. Input fingerprints identify file metadata; original-byte content checksums remain in the bronze ingestion manifest. The analytics tests verify no future-outcome or future-weather leakage, late-report availability, 52-week evaluation coverage, the scoring formula, and actual optimizer constraints. Spark integration fixtures also verify that a delayed backfill is excluded while a promptly reported true rise remains detectable.
